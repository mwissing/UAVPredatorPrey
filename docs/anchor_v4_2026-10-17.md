# Anchor v4 (2026-10-17): obstacle reference pair

Anchor v4 is the reference predator-prey pair for the 10-pillar task (Stage
8), trained with the current architecture. A pre-registered gate accepted
it: competent and stable. It is the baseline that the representation and
credit fixes of the [Stage 8 review](stage8_review_2026-10-17.md) have to
beat.

- Read-only bundle: `.pretrained_checkpoints/anchor_v4_2026-10-17/`
  (gitignored). It holds README, MANIFEST.json, SHA256SUMS.txt, and
  provenance: experiment, evaluations, run parameters, diagnostics.
- Checkpoint: `current/agent_432000.pt`, sha256 `5b4c097f...`. Evaluating
  the bundled copy reproduced the final own-matchup evaluation bit-exactly.
- Task `3v1-survival-soft-oob-teammate-vel-random-spawn-low-progress-obstacles10-v0`,
  preset `...large_gru_obstacles10`, code `4cf40a9`.
- Evaluation needs both masks:
  `+agent.models.policy.prey_mask_inactive_predators=true`
  `+agent.models.policy.predator_mask_inactive_teammates=true`.

## How it was made

1. Anchor v3, expanded to pillars with zero-initialized obstacle branches
   (2026-10-08/10). The predator learned perception-based pillar avoidance
   from a crash price plus exploration.
2. Mixed self-play in the 10-pillar layout, with a third of the envs against
   scripted 3.5 m/s ghosts (`experiments/2026-10-12_mixed_wall_pillars10/`).
   Here the prey learned to wait behind pillars.
3. A predator-only stage against that waiting prey, with predator crash -400
   and the forced-crash bonus 200 (`experiments/2026-10-15_predator_stage_forced_crash/`,
   arm f).
4. The reference run: mixed self-play from arm f's final pair, 18,000
   updates, seed 60, with the obstacle defaults (prey crash -300, predator
   crash -400, bonus 200), noise floor -1.0, and both masks
   (`experiments/2026-10-16_obstacle_reference_selfplay/`).

## Gate and evaluation

The protocol is deterministic, masked, 1024 episodes, seed 42. The fixed
opponents are the obstacle-blind anchor v3 pair.

| | anchor v4 | start (arm f's final pair) |
|---|---:|---:|
| own catch rate | 0.680 | 0.719 |
| own success (catch or forced prey crash) | 0.771 | 0.783 |
| own ablation ratio, prey / predator | 1.25 / 1.23 | 0.93 / 1.10 |
| our predator vs the fixed prey: catch | 0.149 | 0.146 |
| the fixed predator vs our prey: caught | 0.147 | 0.167 |
| time-out share | 0.062 | 0.040 |
| predators lost per episode | 0.37 | 0.29 |
| prey vs 3.5 m/s ghosts: catch hazard | 0.274/s | 0.159/s |

The pre-registered gate passed all six criteria (points 4 and 5):
- perception ratios 1.14 (prey) and 1.22 (predator), mean of points 4 and 5;
- own catch 0.678 / 0.680;
- no forgetting;
- catch change 0.002.

## Caveats

- **The end game is unsolved.** The prey waits behind a pillar. Time-outs
  rose to 6.2% in self-play, and long episodes end by time-out in 55% of
  cases. The predators stay about 6.6 m away there.
- **Measured blind spots:**
  - the predators' one-hot pillar attention, queried by own state only,
    attends the prey's pillar in 0.7% of standoff steps;
  - the prey shares a one-hot softmax with the teammates and is in a
    predator's attention context in 49% of steps;
  - the critic's explained variance is -0.09 in long episodes;
  - frames are mixed, with the heading unobserved, while the drones yaw
    about 125 deg/s.
  These are why no predator variant broke the strategy.
- **The prey's skill is opponent-specific.** Against the obstacle-blind
  fixed predator its perception ratio is 0.86. Against ghosts it got worse.
- **No coordination.** Wall enclosure before catches went 0.21 -> 0.18.
